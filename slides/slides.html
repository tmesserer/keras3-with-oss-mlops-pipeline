Course introduction · 1 of 2

The part of the job your course does not teach

You are about 30% into your specialisation. Data science and machine learning share around 80% of their content. Almost none of that 80% is MLOps.

  • Your courses stop when the model works. They teach data, models, and scores. That is the hard part, and it is not the whole job.
  • A working model is not yet a product. Someone has to serve it, watch it, correct it, and train it again next month.
  • That work has a name: MLOps. Machine learning operations. It is a loop, and you are going to build one twice.
Nothing here replaces your specialisation. This course gives you the frame around it, so the model you build has somewhere to live.
Course introduction · 2 of 2

Two parts. The second is three times the first.

Read the slides first. Run the notebook afterwards, on your own Linux machine. The notebook gives the commands; these slides give the meaning.

Part 1 · handwritten digits

part1.html · 10 slides

  • One tool at the centre: MLflow. It records every training run and holds the catalogue of trained models.
  • Five stages: data, train, select, serve, observe.
  • You close the loop by hand. You read the numbers, you choose the winner, you start the server.
  • The dataset cannot surprise you. That is the point: every surprise then comes from the pipeline.

Part 2 · card fraud

part2.html · 28 slides

  • About three times larger. Seven stages and three loops.
  • An application that depends on the model. A queue where analysts review decisions. A dataset identified by a hash.
  • A scheduler does what your hands did in Part 1: retraining, and collecting human labels.
  • Card transactions, fraud, an analyst in the loop. That is the shape of the problem the finance sector in Luxembourg actually has.
And LLMs? Once this loop exists, a large language model is just another model inside it. It still needs to be served, watched, corrected by people, and replaced by a better version. Part 2 is where you will see exactly which box it would sit in.
